# Does handwriting notes work better than typing them?

_Answered from the wiki on 2026-08-12._

## Answer

**The wiki doesn't cover this.** Nothing in it addresses handwriting, typing, or the
cognitive differences between them — there is no claim here I can point at either way, and
I'm not going to supply one from outside the collection.

What the wiki *does* say sits one level up from the question, and is worth separating from
it. Its claims are about what a note has to contain, not what you write it with:

- A note has to carry one idea, stated as a claim you could defend
  ([Atomic Notes](../wiki/Atomic%20Notes.md)).
- It has to be understandable by a reader who wasn't there — which in eight months is you
  ([Write for the Stranger](../wiki/Write%20for%20the%20Stranger.md)).
- The distillation should happen in layers separated by time, not on first read
  ([Progressive Summarization](../wiki/Progressive%20Summarization.md)).

There's one adjacent claim that might be what you're reaching for: highlighting fails
because it *feels* like work without being work — the yellow bar moves and the brain
registers progress that didn't happen. If the handwriting question is really "does the extra
effort of writing by hand force more processing?", the wiki has a shape for that argument but
never makes it about handwriting specifically. Treat that as a hypothesis the collection
suggests, not a finding it supports.
