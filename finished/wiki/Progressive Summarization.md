# Progressive Summarization

Progressive summarization distills a source in layers separated by time, rather than trying
to extract its value on the first read. First pass: read it, keep the whole thing. Second
pass, weeks later: pull out what still seems to matter. Third pass: rewrite that in your own
words. Each layer is smaller than the one before it.

## Why the first read is the wrong moment to decide

Highlighting fails because it asks you to mark what's important during the one read where
you don't yet know what the material is about. You make the selection at the moment you are
least qualified to make it, and the result is a book where every third sentence is marked —
which conveys nothing, because marking everything is the same as marking nothing.

The activity also feels like work while not being work. Dragging a yellow bar across a page
produces the sensation of progress without the cost of understanding, which is the mechanism
behind [[The Collector's Fallacy]].

## Forgetting is the filter

The gap between passes is not incidental — it is the part that works. Doing all three passes
in one sitting defeats the method, because what does the filtering is your forgetting. An
idea still present in your head three weeks later is signal; one that has evaporated was
decoration.

This removes the judgment call rather than automating it. Instead of interrogating every
paragraph — *is this important? is this?* — which is both exhausting and something people
are unreliable at, you wait and see what survives on its own.

## Structure comes last

The same logic applies to the system around the notes. Building the folder scheme, the tags,
and the templates before there is any material to organize is the same error in a different
costume: imposing a structure at the moment you know least about what it needs to hold. The
categories should be discovered from the material once there's enough of it to show a shape.

What the final layer should look like is covered in [[Write for the Stranger]], and the
granularity it should land at in [[Atomic Notes]].
