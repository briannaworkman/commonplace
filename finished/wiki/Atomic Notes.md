# Atomic Notes

An atomic note contains exactly one idea, written in your own words, titled as a claim you could
defend. The discipline sounds fussy and is easy to dismiss as pedantry, but it is what makes a
note collection linkable at all.

## Why one idea per note

The constraint pays off at the moment of linking. A note about exactly one thing can be pointed at
from anywhere, reused in any argument, and revised without collateral damage to unrelated
material. A note about six things can't be pointed at precisely — there's no way to say "I mean
the third paragraph" — so in practice it never gets linked, and an unlinked note is unreachable.

This is why atomicity and [[Linked Thought]] are the same idea seen from two ends. Links are only
worth making if the things being linked are specific enough to mean something.

## Titles as claims

Titling a note as a defensible claim rather than a topic ("Folders force one location per idea"
rather than "Notes on folders") forces the digestion the system depends on. You cannot write the
title until you have understood the thing, which is the entire point — it makes storing something
you haven't processed slightly harder than processing it.

The same requirement shows up as [[Write for the Stranger]]: a note has to hold up for a reader
who wasn't there, and one idea stated plainly is what clears that bar. Atomicity is the
granularity; writing for the stranger is the self-sufficiency. A note needs both.

## Insertion, not accumulation

In a [[Zettelkasten]], atomicity is what lets new material be inserted next to the idea it
responds to instead of appended to the end of a pile. Luhmann's branching index numbers exist to
serve this: any card can go beside any other card. Granularity is the precondition for that kind
of growth.
